Artemis IV: The Mission That Will Change the Moon

By Maya Chen May 4, 2025 9 min read

When Artemis III touched down at the lunar south pole in late 2026, it made history as humanity's first crewed Moon landing in over 50 years. But that mission — dramatic as it was — was only the beginning. Artemis IV is where the real transformation begins: not just a visit, but the start of a permanent human presence on the Moon.

"Artemis III showed we could return. Artemis IV shows we intend to stay." — Bill Nelson, NASA Administrator

The Gateway: A Space Station Around the Moon

The centrepiece of Artemis IV is the Lunar Gateway — a small modular space station in a near-rectilinear halo orbit (NRHO) around the Moon. Unlike the ISS in low Earth orbit, the Gateway sits in a highly elliptical path that brings it close to the lunar surface at regular intervals, making it an ideal staging post for surface missions.

The first two Gateway modules — the Power and Propulsion Element (PPE) and the Habitation and Logistics Outpost (HALO) — were launched in 2024. Artemis IV's crew will be the first to dock with, enter, and begin operating this outpost. They will add the International Habitation module (I-HAB), contributed by ESA and JAXA, dramatically expanding the station's capability.

The South Pole and Its Secrets

All Artemis landings target the lunar south pole region — and for good reason. Permanently shadowed craters here contain some of the oldest water ice in the solar system, never touched by sunlight since the Moon formed. This is not merely a scientific curiosity. Water ice means oxygen for breathing, hydrogen for rocket fuel, and the raw material for a permanent off-world economy.

  • Confirmed water ice deposits at Shackleton, Haworth, and Nobile craters
  • Up to 600 million metric tonnes of ice estimated in permanently shadowed regions
  • Prospecting robots will accompany Artemis IV to begin mapping usable deposits
  • In-situ resource utilisation (ISRU) demonstrations planned for this mission

Who Is Flying?

NASA has selected a four-person crew for Artemis IV, making history in several respects. Commander Christina Hammock Koch will become the first woman to land on the Moon. Mission Specialist Ven Ramdev of ESA will be the first non-American astronaut to set foot on the lunar surface under the Artemis programme. The crew also includes two NASA astronauts whose expertise spans geology, medicine, and robotics.

"We're not going as explorers who'll come back with samples and stories. We're going as the advance team for civilization." — Christina Hammock Koch

The Bigger Picture: Road to Mars

Every element of the Artemis programme is designed with Mars in mind. The Gateway tests long-duration habitation hardware in deep space. The Orion capsule's life support systems are prototypes for interplanetary spacecraft. The south pole ISRU experiments will validate the resource extraction techniques that any Mars mission will require.

The Moon, in NASA's strategic framework, is not the destination — it is the training ground. If Artemis IV succeeds in demonstrating sustained Gateway operations and in-situ resource use, it will unlock the funding, technology, and political will needed for the 2031–2033 Mars mission window that mission planners are targeting.

The cosmos does not wait. And for the first time in half a century, neither does humanity.
